# DECISIONS — locked, with reasons

One line per locked decision + the REASON. Source of truth; append as things lock.

1. **Nav = gyro heading-hold, 90° turns terminated by IMU, re-reference heading at every corner** — turn ends on measured heading not steering angle, so tyre slip can't corrupt it; re-referencing bounds gyro drift. Mirrors the previous BD national winner (fastest + smoothest).

   - **AMENDED — 2026-07-26 (as-built).** Turn trigger is now the **TCS34725 floor colour sensor**, not the front wall distance. Corner lines are 20 mm thick (rule 13.9); at the as-built 0.70 m/s a line is under the sensor for 28.6 ms, giving ~11 samples at 2.4 ms integration. Front ToF is DEMOTED to confirmation/anti-collision only. Two additions are mandatory and were not in the original decision: (a) **event lockout** — each corner is marked by an orange line AND a blue line, so a naive "saw colour" trigger fires ~24 times over 3 laps instead of 12; all colour input must be ignored from the moment a turn starts until the turn completes and the car has driven clear; (b) **direction decode** — the driving direction is drawn randomly before each round (rule 9.3), so the ORDER of the first line pair (orange-then-blue vs blue-then-orange) sets the turn direction for all 12 turns. Without (b), 50% of runs steer the wrong way. The IMU-terminated 90° turn and per-corner heading re-reference are UNCHANGED and remain the core of the decision.

2. **Compute split: Open round = STM32 only; Obstacle round adds Pi + fisheye for pillar colour only; link = checksummed UART, never inter-board I2C** — keeps the driving loop fully deterministic with no Pi/WiFi jitter; I2C between boards is a known glitch source, UART with checksum is robust.

3. **STM32 over ESP32** — clean 12-bit ADC for analog Sharp IR (ESP32 ADC noise becomes distance randomness), hardware quadrature encoder timers, deterministic timing with no WiFi stack stealing cycles.

4. **Steering = single central pivot, bell-crank, bearing-supported shaft, AS5600 encoder on the shaft, servo commanded in microseconds** — bell-crank is the documented RC standard (turntable swings wheels fore/aft + long lever arm for bump loads); AS5600 closes the loop on true wheel angle and kills servo backlash; µs commanding avoids the ~10 µs/deg quantisation of degree commands.
   - **EVIDENCE 2026-07-12 (single central pivot CONFIRMED for parking):** a kinematic slop Monte-Carlo + a pymunk dynamic-friction sim (`src/sim/`, `journal/steering-study-2026-07-12.md`) compared single central pivot (turntable) vs Ackermann bell-crank vs parallel bell-crank for the parallel park. Findings: (a) equal-angle scrub is 8° at full lock kinematically BUT dynamically cheap at parking speed (surface sensitivity ~equal, TT 2.7 mm vs ACK 2.9 mm); (b) single pivot is ~3× more robust to build slop (0.42 vs 1.25 mm run-to-run @0.5° joint slop) because its one bearinged joint is exactly what the AS5600 measures; (c) 1 joint vs 5 — decisive for a novice assembler. Parallel bell-crank is strictly dominated → dropped. Ackermann only pays off at higher speed (untested) or with downstream joints built <0.3°. Net: single central pivot stays locked for v1.

   - **SUPERSEDED — 2026-07-26 (as-built: parallelogram steering, AS5600 dropped).** The car was built with **parallelogram (equal-angle, tie-bar) steering**, not a single central pivot, and without the AS5600. Reason, in the team's words: a central pivot turns the whole front beam about one axis, so during a turn one front wheel swings forward and the other swings back — the car's effective length grows exactly when clearance matters most. Quantified: each front wheel translates fore/aft by **(track/2)·sin δ**. At the as-built track = 105 mm and lock δ = 35°, that is **±30.1 mm per wheel**, which consumes **36% of the 82.5 mm total parking slack** (bay = 1.5 × 165 mm car = 247.5 mm) for zero navigational benefit, against a rule where touching a magenta parking-lot limitation scores 0 parking points (rule 9.24.7).
     **Why the simulation did not catch this:** `src/sim/steering_study.py` scored candidates on kinematic scrub, parking accuracy and slop robustness. It **never modelled the swept envelope of the vehicle body during the turn**. That missing objective term is the entire reason it ranked parallel bell-crank as "strictly dominated". The build found the term the model was missing. This is recorded as an iteration, not as an error: the original study and its conclusion are preserved unedited in `journal/steering-study-2026-07-12.md`.
     **Accepted costs, on the record:** (a) 4+ linkage joints instead of 1, against `DECISIONS.md` #10's novice-assembler principle; (b) no AS5600, therefore **steering is fully open-loop** — commanded angle ≠ actual angle, and the error will grow as joints wear; (c) equal-angle steering, so tyre scrub is UNCHANGED from the turntable (parallelogram is not Ackermann); (d) **turn radius R is nominal, not measured.**
     **Why it is still sound:** the loop is closed on *vehicle heading* by the IMU, not on steering angle (Decision #1). Steering therefore only has to be monotonic and repeatable, not accurate. This is the same argument that lets Decision #1 terminate turns on measured heading rather than steering angle. The residual exposure is parallel parking, which needs a predictable arc — see Decision #21.

5. **Ackermann rejected for v1** — 4–6 slop-prone joints, worst choice for a novice assembler; analyse the trade-off in the journal for rubric points instead of building it.

6. **"Straight" found by motion, not by eye (sweep µs, drive ~3 m, bisect gyro yaw-rate to zero → CENTER_US)** — no car is mechanically straight; gyro heading-hold finds the true straight, removing a source of randomness.

7. **Sensors = analog Sharp IR (calibrated, median-filtered, mounted out of the fold-back zone) and/or front sonar; ToF rejected; LIDAR deferred** — walls are big flat perpendicular reflectors ideal for IR/sonar; ToF randomly false-detects the floor; LIDAR is heavy/tall and forces Pi 5 + Linux jitter against the determinism goal (design upper deck to accept it later).
   - **REVISION 2026-07-12 (team override):** primary distance = **ToF VL53L1X ×5** (already on hand). This reverses the "ToF rejected" clause above. Reason: parts owned; VL53L1X is far better than the generic ToF the rejection referred to. RISK acknowledged (false floor returns on a low car) → mitigate by above-grazing-angle mount, tight ROI window, min-range + floor gating, median filter, and VALIDATE on the real mat. Sharp IR / sonar demoted to documented fallback (Sharp bought only if ToF fails on the mat). Original rejection reasoning kept above so the trade-off is on record for the journal.

   - **CORRECTED — 2026-07-26 (part is VL53L0X, not VL53L1X).** The sensors on hand are **VL53L0X ×5**, not VL53L1X. This invalidates the primary mitigation assumed in the 2026-07-12 revision: **the VL53L0X has no programmable ROI.** Its field of view is fixed at ~25° and cannot be narrowed in firmware. The mitigation is therefore MECHANICAL — see Decision #18.
     **Why this matters more than it looks:** the mat is white (rule 13.2) and both exterior and interior walls are black on every visible face (rules 13.4, 13.6). The false target (floor) therefore reflects NIR *better* than the true target (wall). A naive closest-return read locks onto the floor. This is the concrete, quantified form of the original "ToF randomly false-detects the floor" objection that this decision overrode, and it is now the single highest-risk item in the build. The Sharp GP2Y0A21 fallback in `BOM.md` must have a confirmed lead time before the mitigation is validated on the mat.

8. **Sensors + camera mount to the FIXED chassis, never the pivoting steering beam** — readings must not swing when the steering turns.

9. **Ball bearings on every axle AND the steering pivot** — plastic-on-shaft wears in and grows slop mid-competition; bearings keep geometry constant.

10. **Locate every part by GEOMETRY (shoulders, slots, captured screws); glue is backup only** — a loose joint must still be unable to shift, because the assembler is a novice and joints sometimes come loose.

11. **No Dupont jumpers / no breadboard on the robot — solder or JST/screw terminals with strain relief** — jumpers/breadboards are the #1 source of "random" glitches.

12. **Separate motor + logic power domains joined at ONE star ground; bulk cap 470–1000 µF on motor rail, 0.1 µF near MCU, 0.1 µF across motor terminals; threadlocker on every thread; twisted encoder leads with pull-ups** — motor brown-out causes random MCU resets and EMI causes random glitches; these kill both.

13. **Deliverable of the design phase = a justified, print-ready v1 — NOT a finished car** — "competition-level" is earned by testing and iterating on a real mat in Sept–Oct, not in chat.

14. **Drivetrain = SOLID rear axle (both rear wheels locked to one shaft); NO open differential** — quantified in `src/sim/rear_axle_scrub.py` / `media/steering/6_rear_axle.png`. A solid axle scrubs 94 mm per rear wheel per 90° corner (= track/2 × angle, independent of radius), BUT distance odometry is **unbiased (0.00 mm) for both a solid axle and a diff** — the encoder sits at the mean-wheel-speed (axle / diff carrier) so scrub cancels; the diff fixes nothing there. An open diff instead ADDS: gear-backlash odometry jitter (±0.17–0.70 mm per reversal) and, on the low-grip mat, a torque-bias one-wheel-spin failure = a randomness event that violates the no-randomness spec, plus more parts/slop for a novice assembler. Net: the diff's only real benefit (no scrub) has no scored payoff for a low-speed, gyro-navigated, sensor-parked car, so the simple solid axle wins. (Revisit only if the car ever becomes fast + grippy + high-cornering.)

   - **CONFIRMED as-built — 2026-07-26.** Built as specified: solid rear axle, no differential, driven through a **5:1 gear reduction** meshed to the rear wheels. The 5:1 was not in the original decision and is the single highest-value change in the build: it takes the 25GA from 1331 RPM / 2.79 m/s (uncontrollable) to **266 RPM / 0.70 m/s**, and multiplies encoder resolution by 5. It also makes the TCS line-detection timing comfortable (28.6 ms dwell on a 20 mm line vs 7.2 ms ungeared).

15. **Steering = parallelogram (equal-angle tie-bar), lock ±35°, open-loop** — as built. Full rationale, quantified swept-envelope argument and accepted costs are in the Decision #4 supersession block above. Measured lock angle 35°; with the wheelbase this gives R = wheelbase ÷ tan 35°. Track 105 mm centre-to-centre, 115 mm wheel-extreme to wheel-extreme.

16. **Steering loop is closed on IMU heading, not on steering angle** — the AS5600 was dropped with the central pivot, so there is no steering-angle feedback. This is acceptable because Decision #1 already terminates turns on measured heading; steering only needs to be monotonic and repeatable. The cost is that turn radius is nominal rather than measured, which is harmless for the 12 open-round corners and is the binding risk for parallel parking (Decision #21).

17. **Turn trigger = TCS34725 floor colour, with hard event lockout and first-pair direction decode** — see the Decision #1 amendment. Front ToF demoted to confirmation. TCS integration time ≤10 ms; **no median filter on the colour sensor** — a median filter smears a 28.6 ms line event away. Use single-sample edge detection with hysteresis.

18. **VL53L0X floor rejection is MECHANICAL: a printed collimator snout, 2.5 mm tall × 10 mm wide × 20 mm deep, plus a +2° upward mounting wedge** — the L0X has no ROI (Decision #7 correction), so firmware cannot narrow the beam. The snout narrows the *vertical* half-angle to atan(1.25/20) = **3.58°** while leaving horizontal FoV intact. The slot must be WIDE, not round: the L0X emitter and receiver apertures are ~2.5 mm apart and a round hole vignettes the receiver.
    **The wedge is not optional.** Front wheels are 46 mm and rear wheels are 50 mm, so the chassis sits nose-down by atan(2/wheelbase) ≈ **1.0–1.1°**, and every chassis-mounted sensor inherits it — aimed at the floor. Net effective half-angle θ_eff = 3.58° + rake − wedge.
    Floor first-return `d_floor = h / tan(θ_eff)`, against a side-wall distance of (1000 − 115)/2 = **442.5 mm**:
    | Wedge | θ_eff | d_floor @ h=40 mm | Margin |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 0° | 4.67° | 490 mm | +47 mm ✗ |
    | +1.1° (cancel rake only) | 3.58° | 640 mm | +197 mm |
    | **+2.0°** | **2.67°** | **859 mm** | **+416 mm ✓** |
    Beam axis at 442.5 mm is then 47 mm high — far below the 100 mm wall top (rules 13.3, 13.5), so nothing is lost. Margin improves as car height grows (see `electrical/collimator.py`). **Offset calibration must be re-run after the snout is fitted** — the hood changes crosstalk.

19. **ToF layout = F / FL30° / FR30° / L90° / R90°, with a deliberate split of duties** — L90/R90 do open-corridor lane centring (442.5 mm lateral, well inside d_floor). FL30/FR30 do *obstacle-corridor* centring when threading a pillar-to-wall gap, where slant range = gap ÷ sin 30° = 2 × gap: a 150 mm gap reads at 300 mm, a 350 mm gap at 700 mm — both comfortably inside d_floor. The 30° pair degrades exactly when the gap is wide, which is when L90/R90 take over. **Known risk to bench-test:** at 30° off-axis the beam strikes the wall at 60° from normal, so Lambertian return falls by cos 60° = 0.5 on a surface that is already black. Verify signal rate against black MDF at 60° incidence at 300 mm and 700 mm before committing mount angles.

20. **Bus split: 5× VL53L0X alone on I²C1 (XSHUT-reassigned to 0x30–0x34); TCS34725 alone on I²C2; MPU9250 on SPI1** — the VL53L0X and the TCS34725 share factory address **0x29**, and the TCS has neither a shutdown pin nor an address strap. During ToF enumeration each sensor wakes at 0x29, so every enumeration write would also hit the colour sensor. Splitting the buses removes the collision and additionally isolates the turn trigger — the single most critical sensor in the open round — from any ToF cable fault. Moving the MPU9250 to SPI (it is SPI-capable) leaves the TCS as the sole device on I²C2.
    **XSHUT is open-drain, no internal pull-up** — the L0X die is 2.8 V logic; pull low to shut down, release to Hi-Z and let the breakout's own pull-up set the rail. Safe on 2.8 V-native and 3.3 V-shifted boards alike.
    **Reassigned addresses are volatile and are lost on any brownout**, so enumeration re-runs on every reset: all XSHUT low → 10 ms → release ch0, 2 ms, write 0x30 → repeat to 0x34 → verify → **skip any failure and continue**, never stall → report result on the status LED before entering ARMED.
    **Do not use the MPU9250 magnetometer.** A BTS7960 switching amps on a 115 mm-wide chassis will corrupt it. Gyro-Z only, DLPF ≈ 41 Hz, polled on a fixed 1 kHz timer tick for a constant integration timestep.

21. **Parallel parking arcs are closed on IMU yaw, not on odometry or steering angle** — steering is open-loop (Decision #16), so the nominal R cannot be trusted to produce a repeatable arc. Closing each park arc on measured yaw uses the sensor already trusted for the 12 corners and removes the dependence on R entirely. Feasibility against the two magenta limiters must be proven by `src/sim/park_feasibility.py` before mat testing; touching a limiter is 0 parking points (rule 9.24.7).
    - **RESULT 2026-07-26 — the two-arc park FAILS and the manoeuvre is changed.** The solver sweeps the parked longitudinal position (the only free variable at fixed lock) and finds the front-outer corner strikes the entry limiter by **25.6 mm** at the as-built 35 deg lock. R/L = 0.95; a symmetric two-arc needs roughly **R/L <= 0.7**. Raising lock to 45 deg reaches R/L = 0.67 and clears by ~0.1 mm — no usable margin, and worthless with open-loop steering. **The problem is scale-invariant:** bay = 1.5 x car length always, so slack = 0.5 x car length always; shortening the car cannot help. **Primary parking manoeuvre is therefore a MULTI-POINT (3+) SHUFFLE closed on IMU yaw, not a two-arc.** This supersedes "Park manoeuvre: reverse two-arc" as carried in SPECSHEET since 2026-07-12.

22. **Motor driver = BTS7960, accepted with its costs stated** — on hand, and it will work. It is ~20× oversized for a 25GA (43 A rating), weighs ~66 g with heatsink against a ~450 g target, and occupies 50 × 50 × 27 mm on an 80 × 130 mm plate alongside a Pi 4B. Its real functional cost is poor resolution at the bottom of the range, exactly where parking lives (~0.1 m/s ≈ 14% duty). **Closed-loop speed control on the encoder is therefore mandatory, not optional.** At 0.175 mm/count there is ample feedback to do it. A TB6612FNG would be a better match on every axis except headroom; revisit if low-speed control proves erratic on the mat.
